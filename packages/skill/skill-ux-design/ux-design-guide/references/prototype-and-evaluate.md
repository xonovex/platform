# Prototype and Evaluate

## Choose Fidelity by Risk

Use sketches for structure and breadth, wireflows for sequence and state, content
prototypes for comprehension, and interactive prototypes only when realistic behavior
or timing matters. A prototype should answer named questions; omit production
qualities unrelated to those questions and label the omissions.

Do not copy disposable prototype code into production by default. Protect participant
data and restrict access when a prototype could be mistaken for a live service.

## Evaluation Contract

For each design hypothesis, define:

- representative participants and relevant access needs;
- realistic task, starting context, and success condition;
- observable behaviors and breakdowns;
- confounds and prototype limitations;
- evidence that would retain, revise, or reject the approach.

Pair evaluative research with accessibility review and technical feasibility evidence
where those risks matter. Do not turn a small usability sample into a population
percentage.

## Synthesis

Trace each finding to observations and an exact prototype revision. Distinguish
critical task failure, recurring friction, comprehension problems, preference, and
facilitator or prototype artifacts. Revise the design hypothesis, state model, or
problem frame when the evidence warrants it, then identify what remains untested.
