# Experiments and Evidence

1. Inventory assumptions about desirability, usability, feasibility, viability,
   adoption, operations, policy, and measurement.
2. Score each assumption by consequence if false and current evidence weakness.
3. Choose the highest-consequence weakly supported assumption that can change the
   decision.
4. Define the question, target participants or system, observable signal, threshold,
   guardrails, time box, and decision that each possible result changes.
5. Select the smallest method that produces credible evidence: existing-data review,
   interview or observation, usability test, prototype, technical spike, operational
   rehearsal, or limited experiment.
6. Record observations separately from interpretations and retain contradictory or
   negative evidence.
7. End when the decision threshold is met, the time box expires, or new evidence makes
   another assumption riskier.

Do not design an experiment that can only confirm the preferred intervention. Include
a result that would cause change, pause, or stop. Protect participant privacy and
avoid exposing users to production risk merely to accelerate learning.
