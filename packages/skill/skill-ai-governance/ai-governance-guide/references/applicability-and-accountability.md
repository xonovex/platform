# Applicability and Accountability

## Produce an applicability result before a regulated profile

Resolve and record:

- jurisdiction, territorial reach, effective dates, transition periods, and selected current legal or policy text;
- intended purpose, actual use, capabilities, autonomy, affected decisions, users, subjects, and operating context;
- value-chain roles such as provider, deployer, importer, distributor, product manufacturer, authorized representative, or affected operator;
- candidate prohibited, high-risk, transparency, general-purpose, sector-specific, or exempt classifications;
- entity, product, system, sector, organization-size, research, open-source, national-security, or personal-use scope where relevant;
- confirmed facts, assumptions, unresolved questions, candidate obligations, evidence, source version, reviewer, and review date.

Unknown classification or role fails visibly when a profile claims a regulated guarantee.

## Keep claim authority explicit

Distinguish:

- deterministic facts from authoritative configuration, artifacts, records, and providers;
- model or agent inferences and recommendations;
- automated evaluation evidence;
- qualified domain, security, privacy, accessibility, or legal review;
- accountable organizational decisions and external authority.

An agent drafts candidate mappings; it cannot replace licensed text, qualified legal advice, a notified or accredited assessment, organizational accountability, or regulator authority.

## Map frameworks without claiming equivalence

Every crosswalk entry records source and target version, relationship strength, scope, rationale, evidence expectation, gaps, conflicts, assumptions, reviewer, and date. Use mappings to organize work, never to transform a framework outcome into automatic compliance with another framework, standard, contract, or law.

When full standard text is licensed, record the selected edition and keep restricted content in its authorized system. Public abstracts support high-level applicability only, not clause-level certification claims.

## Assign accountable ownership

Name the system owner, risk owner, data owner, model owner, control owners, evaluation owner, incident owner, change authority, human-oversight roles, and independent reviewers. Define segregation of duties and escalation for conflicting commercial, delivery, safety, privacy, security, accessibility, and legal goals.

Accountability remains with the authorized organization and people even when providers, models, or agents execute parts of the work.
