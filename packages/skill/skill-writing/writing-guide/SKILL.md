# Writing Guidelines

Write clear prose that preserves meaning and lets the reader verify each claim.

## Essentials

- **Give each sentence one job** - Use an active subject and a concrete verb, see [references/plain-prose.md](references/plain-prose.md)
- **Keep names stable** - Use one term for one thing and make every reference unambiguous, see [references/plain-prose.md](references/plain-prose.md)
- **Keep evidence with its claim** - State comparison conditions and distinguish fact from inference, see [references/plain-prose.md](references/plain-prose.md)
- **Control the detail** - Retain what the reader needs and remove filler, narration, and duplicate summaries, see [references/plain-prose.md](references/plain-prose.md)

## Gotchas

- Clear prose does not require every sentence to have the same length. Preserve rhythm when the medium needs it.
- Do not replace a precise ordinary word with a synonym only to avoid repetition.
- Do not invent a fact, source, owner, deadline, action, or confidence level while tightening prose.
- An artifact-specific skill owns required structure, voice, terminology controls, and publication format. Apply these rules within that contract.
