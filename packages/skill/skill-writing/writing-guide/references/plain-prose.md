# plain-prose: Write Clear and Verifiable Prose

## Make Each Sentence Do One Job

- Use one main idea per sentence.
- Prefer an active subject and a concrete verb: "The validator rejects an empty token" instead of "An empty token is subject to rejection."
- Split stacked conditions and consequences. Keep the condition beside the result it controls.
- Remove setup narration such as "This section discusses" and start with the subject.

## Keep Terms and References Stable

- Use one term for one thing. Do not alternate between synonyms when they can look like different concepts.
- Define unavoidable jargon at first use. Remove jargon that adds no precision.
- Preserve exact names, identifiers, dates, units, and labels when they matter.
- Make every pronoun and shortened reference point to one clear subject.
- Do not invent a label or abbreviation only to shorten one document.

## Connect Claims to Evidence

- Put measurements, test results, sources, or observed details beside the claim they support.
- Mark an inference as an inference. Do not present a plausible explanation as a verified cause.
- State the relevant comparison and conditions for a number. "Sign-ups rose 20 percent" is incomplete without the baseline or measurement period.
- Keep a limitation near the claim when it changes how the reader should interpret the evidence.

## Control Detail

- Match detail to the reader's decision or task. Include what the reader needs to understand or verify the message.
- Use a list for parallel items, a table for repeated-field comparison, and prose for a short linear explanation.
- Remove duplicated summaries, filler transitions, process diaries, and background that does not change the message.
- Preserve the requested register and medium unless the user asks to change them.

## Check

- Does each paragraph have one purpose?
- Does each pronoun have an unambiguous referent?
- Are terms, dates, units, tense, and certainty consistent?
- Can any sentence be deleted without losing meaning or evidence?
