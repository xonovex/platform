# Plan and Recruit

1. State the product or design decision the research must inform.
2. Convert assumptions into answerable research questions and prioritize those that
   can change the decision.
3. Review existing research, analytics, support evidence, complaints, and known
   accessibility gaps before collecting more data.
4. Choose the least burdensome method that can answer the question: interview,
   observation, contextual inquiry, diary study, concept test, or usability test.
5. Define recruitment criteria from relevant behavior, journey stage, environment,
   device, language, confidence, access need, and exclusion risk.
6. Record who the sample cannot represent and how that limits decisions.
7. Prepare accessible participation options, informed consent, compensation, data
   minimization, storage access, retention, withdrawal, and distress handling.
8. Pilot the guide and materials before the first counted session.

Do not recruit only convenient internal users for external-user questions. Do not
collect sensitive demographic or session data without a research purpose and a
retention boundary.
