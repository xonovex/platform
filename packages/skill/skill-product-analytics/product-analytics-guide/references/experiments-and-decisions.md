# Experiments and Decisions

## Match Method to Claim

Descriptive analysis states what happened; diagnostic analysis explores associations;
predictive analysis estimates future observations; causal analysis estimates the
effect of an intervention under explicit assumptions. Use a randomized controlled
experiment when feasible and ethical for a causal product claim. Otherwise document
the design, confounding risks, and weaker conclusion.

Before an experiment, define hypothesis and mechanism, eligibility, randomization unit,
allocation, exposure, primary metric, guardrails, sample or precision rationale,
minimum duration, interference and novelty risks, quality checks, stopping policy, and
decision rule. Avoid changing these after seeing outcomes without labeling the work
exploratory.

## Analysis

Validate assignment, sample ratio, exposure, event quality, attrition, overlap, and
guardrails before estimating effect. Report absolute and relative effects, uncertainty,
practical threshold, important segment heterogeneity, multiple-comparison handling,
and limitations. Do not stop merely when a significance threshold is crossed.

## Decision Record

Connect the observed result to the original mechanism and threshold. Recommend keep,
revise, expand, investigate, or stop with guardrails, qualitative evidence,
unresolved uncertainty, and follow-up measurement. The accountable product owner makes
and persists the decision; analytics preserves evidence and counterevidence.
