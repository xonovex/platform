## What a seed is

A seed is the raw material of a later plan for one coherent chunk of remaining work. It is written into the close-out and persists nothing else. **writing-plan** or **researching-change** turns a seed into a plan in a new change, and the seed carries enough that the next author needs no memory of this one. Use [../assets/templates/follow-up-seed.md](../assets/templates/follow-up-seed.md).

## What a seed holds

- The objective in one sentence.
- Why it exists, and whether the work was attempted and did not land. Work that failed once needs planning, not improvisation.
- The current state, evidenced: each fact with the command or file and the revision.
- What is open: the questions planning must settle.
- Known traps: failure modes already met and how they showed themselves.
- A sketch of the success criteria, each observable by a stated means.
- The validation: the commands or checks that will show the criteria hold.
- What gates it and who owns the gate.
- The skills the future plan should list in `skills_to_consult`.

## Cutting the seeds

- One seed for each coherent chunk: remaining work that shares an outcome, an owner and a gate.
- Every `gated` and `open` criterion, every register entry that waits on work, and every open point that needs work lands in a seed. A criterion no seed covers is an orphan.
- A delivery that is prepared and not applied needs no seed: the user commits and publishes it, and the gated criterion names that gate.
- Do not bundle unrelated leftovers into one seed to keep the count small, and do not split one outcome across seeds.
- A seed never starts the work and never edits the plan. The plan changes through **revising-plan** when the owners agree.

## Sequencing

When there is more than one seed, a table at the top states for each seed whether it starts now or waits for a gate, and which seeds are independent of each other. Order the seeds by what unblocks the most.
